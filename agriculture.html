CAVIMA Agriculture

People, knowledge and practical action.

CAVIMA promotes agricultural knowledge that can help farmers and organizations improve production, manage resources responsibly, reduce avoidable losses and explore value-adding opportunities.

Crop production

Training may cover land preparation, quality planting materials, nursery management, spacing, crop husbandry, irrigation, pest and disease management, harvesting and post-harvest handling.

Horticulture and kitchen gardens

Vegetable and fruit production can contribute to household nutrition and income. CAVIMA can support practical learning in nursery establishment, crop management, water use and basic production planning.

Soil health

Healthy soils support productive agriculture. Learning areas may include soil conservation, organic matter, erosion control, appropriate fertility management, mulching and responsible land management.

Water and irrigation

Efficient water use is essential where water is limited. CAVIMA promotes awareness of water harvesting, storage, efficient irrigation and careful scheduling according to crop and local conditions.

Livestock and integrated farming

Diversification can strengthen household livelihoods. Depending on local priorities, training interests may include poultry, dairy, goats, beekeeping, aquaculture and integrated crop-livestock systems.

Post-harvest handling

Good handling after harvest can protect quality and reduce losses. Topics may include cleaning, sorting, grading, drying, storage, packaging, food safety and basic record keeping.

Value addition and markets

CAVIMA encourages producers to think beyond raw production. Processing, packaging, branding, quality, pricing, market information and customer needs can help communities explore greater value from agricultural products.

Food security and nutrition

Diverse household production, preservation, kitchen gardens and nutrition-sensitive agriculture can strengthen access to nutritious foods while supporting livelihood resilience.

Work with CAVIMA

We welcome responsible conversations with community organizations, farmers, researchers, institutions and development partners interested in practical and sustainable community development.
