Rooted in Ntujia, Marimanti Ward, Tharaka South, Tharaka Nithi County, CAVIMA promotes practical, inclusive and sustainable community action.

CAVIMA is a community-based, non-political and non-profit organization established for lawful community development purposes.

Our community commitment

We combine local knowledge with practical training, responsible partnerships and evidence-informed action. Our aim is to strengthen community capacity so that people can participate in solutions, protect natural resources and pursue resilient livelihoods.

Farmers as decision-makers

CAVIMA treats farmers as partners rather than passive beneficiaries. Their experience and choices are central to programme design.

Production knowledge

Farmers can access practical learning on crop husbandry, livestock opportunities, soil health, water management, pest awareness and post-harvest handling.

Farmer groups

Well-organized groups can improve peer learning, collective purchasing, savings, marketing conversations and representation. CAVIMA can support group organization and practical governance.

Farm records

Simple records can help farmers understand costs, yields, sales, inputs and household production. Better records support more informed decisions.

Enterprise thinking

Agriculture can be approached as a livelihood enterprise while still protecting household food needs. Planning should consider production costs, demand, quality, risk and cash flow.

Market readiness

CAVIMA encourages farmers to understand buyer requirements, quality expectations, timing, aggregation possibilities and value addition opportunities without making unrealistic promises about prices.

Resilience and confidence

Empowerment also means confidence to ask questions, test appropriate ideas, learn from results and work collectively for community priorities.

Working with CAVIMA

We welcome constructive engagement with farmers, community groups, schools, youth and women groups, researchers, volunteers, government extension structures, civil society organizations, private-sector actors and development partners. Any formal collaboration is subject to appropriate agreement, roles, approvals and documentation.
